# V001 V3.1 — Narration

Voice direction: calm, neutral, conversational. This is the V2 direction that tested better than the more dramatic V3 narration.

> Amazon agreed to pay two point five billion dollars to settle FTC allegations that Prime customers were enrolled without consent and made it hard to cancel.
>
> The interesting part isn't just the number. It's the asymmetry.
>
> Joining a subscription is designed to feel instant. Leaving can be very different.
>
> In a separate lawsuit, the FTC alleges some Uber One users had to navigate as many as twenty-three screens and thirty-two actions to cancel.
>
> Why would an exit need that much design?
>
> Because recurring revenue has one weakness: churn. Every customer who doesn't leave keeps the loop alive.
>
> Regulators call some of these tactics dark patterns.
>
> But look at the machine: money in is optimized for speed; money out can be optimized for friction.
>
> That makes the cancel button more than customer service. It can become part of the revenue system.

## Legal/editorial wording

- Amazon: "settle FTC allegations" / settlement is real.
- Uber: "the FTC alleges" / complaint remains allegation.
- Do not state the 2024 Click-to-Cancel amendments are currently in force.
